# Does legal professional privilege in EU competition law cover advice from a company's own in-house lawyers? How have the courts justified where the line falls, and what does that mean in practice during an inspection?

**1.** Legal professional privilege under EU competition law, as established in AM&S Europe v Commission (Case 155/79), protects written communications between a lawyer and client only where the communication is made for the purposes of the client's rights of defence and emanates from an independent lawyer not bound to the client by a relationship of employment.
    61979CJ0155.md

**2.** The independence requirement rests on the lawyer's role as a collaborator in the administration of justice who must give legal assistance in full independence.
    61979CJ0155.md

**3.** Although the Commission's investigative powers under Article 14 of Regulation No 17 extend to requiring production of business documents including lawyer-client communications, that power is restricted so that only communications exchanged with an independent, non-employed lawyer are protected from disclosure.
    61979CJ0155.md

**4.** In Akzo Nobel Chemicals and Akcros Chemicals v Commission (Case C-550/07 P), the Court of Justice held that an in-house lawyer, despite Bar enrolment and professional ethical obligations, does not enjoy the same degree of independence from his employer as an external lawyer enjoys from his client, and is consequently less able to reconcile conflicts between his professional obligations and his employer's aims, a conclusion case commentary traces to the in-house lawyer's economic dependence and close ties with the employer and which a further policy-brief commentary confirms rests on the employment relationship rather than on Member State practice.
    129913.md, 45408-the-eu-court-of-justice-holds-that-in-house-lawyers-are-not-protected-by-legal-p.md, 62007CJ0550.md

**5.** In Akzo Nobel Chemicals v Commission (Case T-125/03), the General Court concluded that the correspondence between Akcros Chemicals' General Manager and a member of Akzo Nobel's legal department was correctly found by the Commission not to be covered by legal professional privilege.
    62003TJ0125.md

**6.** Member State law can diverge from the EU standard, since Dutch law protects communications with in-house lawyers who are admitted to the Dutch Bar and whose employment contract guarantees their professional independence, a protection that does not exist under EU competition law.
    65144.md, competition-inspections-25-jurisdictions--16_netherlands.md

**7.** Similarly, the United Kingdom is among the Member States that extend privilege to in-house counsel's communications, even though the Court of Justice's Akzo Nobel ruling does not itself bind such national proceedings.
    65468.md

**8.** Commentary on EU antitrust enforcement reasons that confining privilege to external lawyers serves inspection effectiveness, since staff communications to in-house lawyers, more than the lawyers' own opinions, regularly contain useful factual evidence of suspected infringements.
    eu-antitrust-enforcement--14_xiii_legal_professional_privilege_in_eu_antitrust_enforcement_law_policy_and_procedure.md

**9.** An Article 20 inspection decision under Regulation 1/2003 takes effect, and the company's duty to cooperate with the Commission arises, only upon notification to the company.
    inspections-eu-law-2nd--3_chapter_1_-_inspections_upon_decision_regulation_1_2003_article_20_4.md

**10.** In Alcogroup and Alcodis v Commission (Case C-403/18 P), the Court of Justice recorded the General Court's observation that judicial review of the conditions in which a Commission inspection was conducted may, in principle, be sought through an action for annulment of the Commission's final decision, while also noting that an action for non-contractual liability remained open where a separate investigation had been closed.
    62018CJ0403.md

**11.** Taken together, AM&S Europe v Commission (Case 155/79) and Akzo Nobel Chemicals and Akcros Chemicals v Commission (Case C-550/07 P) establish that legal professional privilege in EU competition law does not extend to advice from a company's own in-house lawyers, so that during a Commission inspection only communications with independent, externally practising lawyers are shielded from production, with disputed documents provisionally preserved through the sealed-envelope procedure pending resolution of the dispute.
    61979CJ0155.md, 62007CJ0550.md, eu-antitrust-enforcement--14_xiii_legal_professional_privilege_in_eu_antitrust_enforcement_law_policy_and_procedure.md

**12.** The Court of Justice's later ruling in Orde van Vlaamse Balies (Case C-694/20) held that EU legal professional privilege, grounded in Article 7 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights, extends beyond the defence-of-proceedings context recognised in AM&S to cover legal advice more generally, without disturbing AM&S's requirement that the lawyer be independent of the client.
    120985.md

**13.** In practice, a company should raise a privilege claim with Commission inspectors before they examine the document in question and, if inspectors persist and the company has raised serious doubts as to the document's privileged nature, request that it be placed in a sealed envelope pending resolution of the dispute.
    competition-inspections-25-jurisdictions--7_european_union.md
