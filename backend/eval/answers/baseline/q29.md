# Can an inspection decision be annulled in part where the Commission's evidence supports only some of the suspected infringement period it covers, and what then happens to material already gathered?

**1.** An inspection decision may lawfully be adopted only where the Commission has reasonable grounds for suspecting an infringement of the competition rules, and the terms of that decision must not exceed the scope of the infringement which may be suspected on the basis of those grounds.
    inspections-eu-law-2nd--3_chapter_1_-_inspections_upon_decision_regulation_1_2003_article_20_4.md

**2.** The decision must also specify the subject matter and purpose of the investigation, a fundamental requirement that justifies the investigation, lets the undertaking assess the scope of its duty to cooperate and safeguards the rights of the defence.
    inspections-eu-law-2nd--3_chapter_1_-_inspections_upon_decision_regulation_1_2003_article_20_4.md

**3.** The applicants in Nexans France SAS and Nexans SA v Commission (T-135/09) submitted that the Commission held information only about the underwater cable sector yet had extended the inspection to all electric cables and materials, characterising this as an unsupported 'fishing expedition'.
    62009TJ0135.md

**4.** The General Court, in Nexans France SAS and Nexans SA v Commission (T-135/09), annulled the Commission's inspection decision insofar as it concerned electric cables other than high voltage underwater and underground electric cables and the material associated with those other cables, while dismissing the action as to the remainder.
    62009TJ0135.md

**5.** Once the inspection decision's scope is confined in this way, the Commission is required to restrict its searches to the activities of the undertaking relating to the sectors properly covered, and must refrain from using any document or item of information that examination shows falls outside that scope.
    62009TJ0135.md

**6.** The General Court in Deutsche Bahn and Others v Commission (T-289/11, T-290/11 and T-521/11) found that, because the third inspection decision was based in part on information gathered during the first inspection, the conditions under which that earlier information was obtained were capable of affecting the legality of the second and third inspection decisions; the Court of Justice in Deutsche Bahn and Others v Commission (C-583/13 P) set aside the General Court's judgment insofar as it had dismissed the actions against the second and third inspection decisions and annulled those two decisions.
    62011TJ0289.md, 62013CJ0583.md

**7.** Likewise, a finding that an investigation was conducted irregularly, made in an action against the Commission's final infringement decision, precludes use of the evidence so obtained for the purposes of those infringement proceedings.
    judicial-review-competition-cases--6_european_union_-_judicial_review_of_inspection_decisions.md

**8.** In an Advocate General's Opinion, it is explained that during an inspection the Commission may search for, place in the file, and later use as evidence only documents relevant to the subject matter and purpose stated in the inspection decision, so as to ensure that no evidence obtained in breach of the rights of defence is used in subsequent cartel proceedings.
    62018CC0606.md

**9.** The Court of Justice held in Nexans France and Nexans v Commission (C-606/18 P) that the Commission may take copies of data as an intermediate step in its examination under Article 20(2)(b) of Regulation No 1/2003, provided that data are assessed for relevance to the inspection's subject matter before being placed in the file and that the remainder is deleted once that assessment is complete, a limitation also addressed in the Advocate General's Opinion in the same case.
    62018CC0606.md, 62018CJ0606.md

**10.** Illustrating the practical consequence for gathered material, the Markets Court of the Brussels Court of Appeal, in Proximus (2020), reviewed a partially annulled decision of the Belgian competition authority by asking whether the underlying dawn raid had been indispensable to obtaining the remaining evidence, since only in the absence of indispensability could that evidence stay in the file.
    competition-case-law-digest-5th--p10.md

**11.** In Nexans France SAS and Nexans SA v Commission (T-135/09), the General Court's disposition annulled the inspection decision as to electric cables other than high voltage underwater and underground cables and associated material, and rejected the application as to the remainder.
    62009TJ0135.md

**12.** On appeal in Casino, Guichard-Perrachon and AMC v Commission (C-690/20 P), the Court of Justice set aside the General Court's partial dismissal in Casino, Guichard-Perrachon and AMC v Commission (T-249/17) and annulled the inspection decision in its entirety, holding that the unrecorded supplier interviews had vitiated the indicia underlying the whole decision.
    62020CJ0690.md

**13.** In České dráhy v Commission (T-325/16), the General Court likewise annulled an Article 20(4) inspection decision insofar as it concerned routes other than the Prague-Ostrava route and conduct other than the alleged predatory pricing practices, because the Commission lacked reasonable grounds for the broader scope.
    62016TJ0325.md

**14.** In Casino, Guichard-Perrachon and AMC v Commission (T-249/17), the General Court examined whether the Commission possessed sufficiently serious indicia to suspect each of the distinct infringements covered by the inspection decision, and annulled the decision only insofar as it ordered an inspection concerning the applicants' possible participation in the second infringement, dismissing the action as to the remainder.
    62017TJ0249.md
