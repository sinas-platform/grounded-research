# Does the obstruction regime of Article 23(1) of Regulation 1/2003 — conceived for physical records and corporate email — still fit an environment of ephemeral, encrypted messaging on employees' personal devices? Where does the deletion of a WhatsApp or Signal message during or ahead of an inspection cross from ordinary personal housekeeping into a sanctionable procedural infringement, and how must intent and attribution be established when the device and the account belong to the individual rather than the undertaking?

**1.** Obstruction of Commission investigations has drawn increasing enforcement attention worldwide, as shown by Hong Kong's first criminal case for non-compliance with investigation powers, in which an employee was sentenced to two months in prison for attempting to delete documents and computer links that might have been relevant to the Commission's inquiry, by intensified US scrutiny of data management practices as messaging apps with automatic-deletion features become more common in business use, by a Finnish court's €1.5 million fine on a care home service provider whose manager deleted WhatsApp messages and a call log from a personal mobile device during an active investigation despite instructions not to do so, and by the Commission's €15.9 million fine on an international fragrances company after a senior employee deleted incriminating WhatsApp messages during a dawn raid; a separate twenty-year evaluation of Regulation 1/2003 is examining whether the Commission's inspection powers under Article 20(1) remain fit for the digital age.

**2.** The General Court, in Case T-272/12 EPH and EP Investment Advisors v Commission, recalled that the Court of Justice in E.ON Energie v Commission (EU:C:2012:738) had characterized a breach compromising the integrity of evidence gathered during an inspection as particularly serious by its own nature.

**3.** In the Fragrances decision, the Commission found that an IFF employee deleted WhatsApp messages from his mobile phone after learning he was a target of the inspection, and the phone was then submitted for imaging without disclosing the deletion.
    AT.40882-DEC_386A488E-0D8B-4BFE-A3FB-8FCEB16AADAA_-ATT376.md

**4.** In its decision in Case AT.40882 (IFF – deletion of data), the Commission reasoned that 'other records' in Article 23(1)(c) is phrased broadly and covers any storage medium, so that business-related WhatsApp data on a mobile device used for professional purposes falls within the provision.
    AT.40882-DEC_386A488E-0D8B-4BFE-A3FB-8FCEB16AADAA_-ATT376.md

**5.** In Case AT.40882 (IFF – deletion of data), the Commission found that the deletion was intentional because the relevant employee already knew about the investigation before he deleted the information, the inspectors having notified the inspection decision to IFF beforehand.
    AT.40882-DEC_386A488E-0D8B-4BFE-A3FB-8FCEB16AADAA_-ATT376.md

**6.** In its decision in Case AT.40882 (Fragrances), the Commission stated that it is for the undertaking to take all necessary measures to implement the inspectors' instructions and to ensure that persons authorised to act on its behalf do not impede those instructions.
    AT.40882-DEC_386A488E-0D8B-4BFE-A3FB-8FCEB16AADAA_-ATT376.md

**7.** The Netherlands Authority for Consumers and Markets fined a company EUR 1.84 million after employees left WhatsApp groups and deleted chat conversations during a dawn raid despite being told not to destroy evidence, reducing the fine by 20 percent because the company proactively disclosed the conduct and cooperated beyond what was required.

**8.** The Commission classified the Fragrances deletion as a "very serious" obstruction infringement because the employee deleted messages after being informed of the inspection and because the Commission itself detected the deletion rather than being told of it by the company.
    119378.md

**9.** Article 23(1)(c) of Regulation 1/2003 may be infringed either intentionally or negligently, as illustrated by a Commission decision finding negligence as to an unblocked email account and intent as to the diversion of incoming emails in the same case.
    at39793.md

**10.** The same Commission decision reasoned that because electronic files are much easier and quicker to destroy than paper files, it is indispensable that information stored or communicated on an electronic medium, including information in e-mail accounts, be made inaccessible to the account holder during the inspection while remaining accessible to inspectors.
    at39793.md

**11.** In Case AT.40882 (IFF – deletion of data), the Commission's findings together show that deletion of WhatsApp messages becomes sanctionable once it occurs after the employee has learned of the inspection and is found to have acted intentionally, with liability for that conduct attributed to IFF France and IFF Inc. through the conduct of their employee.
    AT.40882-DEC_386A488E-0D8B-4BFE-A3FB-8FCEB16AADAA_-ATT376.md

**12.** In 2022 the European Commission opened a review of Regulation 1/2003 to assess whether its investigation toolbox still provides effective means to investigate infringements in a more digital economy, a review undertaken twenty years into a Regulation whose decentralised enforcement system and European Competition Network have otherwise been assessed as a major success by commentators examining its first two decades.
    129021.md, eu-antitrust-enforcement--2_i_regulation_1_2003_an_assessment_after_twenty_years.md

**13.** Commentary on the Commission's inspection practice under Regulation 1/2003 records that the Commission considers an undertaking responsible for an employee's obstructive conduct even where the employee acted without instructions, requiring it to employ, instruct and control staff so none of them impedes an inspection.
