# To what extent may an undertaking be fined for the breaking of a seal in the absence of any fault or intent, and how is that reconciled with the presumption of innocence?

**1.** In Case T-141/08, E.ON Energie AG v Commission, the General Court recorded that the Commission's contested decision did not proceed on an assumption of intentional infringement but found that the seal had been broken, at the very least, through negligence.
    62008TJ0141.md

**2.** In that same E.ON Energie judgment, the mere fact that the seal is broken nullifies its safeguarding effect and is therefore sufficient in itself to constitute the infringement.
    62008TJ0141.md

**3.** The Commission's decision against E.ON found that the seal had been broken, at the very least, as a result of negligence because it was E.ON's responsibility to organise its business sphere to comply with the instruction not to break the seal, without being able to prove intentional conduct.
    35263.md

**4.** In Case T-141/08, the General Court confirmed that the burden of proving that a breach of seal occurred rests on the Commission, although it need not prove that the sealed room was actually entered.
    62008TJ0141.md

**5.** In Case C-89/11 P, E.ON Energie AG v Commission, the Court of Justice held that once the Commission relies on evidence in principle sufficient to demonstrate the infringement, it is for the undertaking to prove both the existence of any exculpatory circumstance and that it undermines the probative value of that evidence.
    62011CJ0089.md

**6.** In that appeal the Court of Justice held, applying by analogy the judgment in Montecatini v Commission, that requiring E.ON Energie to adduce evidence challenging the Commission's finding did not unduly reverse the burden of proof or set aside the presumption of innocence.
    62011CJ0089.md

**7.** The Court of Justice, in Case C-235/92 P, Montecatini v Commission, judgment of 8 July 1999, had already ruled that such a reversal of the burden of proof does not infringe the principle of the presumption of innocence.
    50125.md

**8.** In Case C-89/11 P, E.ON Energie AG v Commission, the Court of Justice recorded that the General Court, in the judgment under appeal, had observed that a court cannot conclude that the Commission proved an infringement to the requisite legal standard while it still entertained doubt, consistent with the presumption of innocence under Article 6(2) of the European Convention on Human Rights and Article 47 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights.
    62011CJ0089.md

**9.** Regulation No 1/2003 provides that the Commission may fine an undertaking for breach of a seal affixed under Article 20(2)(d), whether caused intentionally or negligently, up to 1% of total turnover in the preceding business year.
    50125.md

**10.** In the E.ON Energie proceeding, a fine of EUR 38,000,000, representing 0.14% of the undertaking's annual turnover, was confirmed by the General Court as not excessive in view of the need to ensure the fine's deterrent effect.
    50125.md

**11.** French law governing obstruction under Article L. 464-2 V of the Commercial Code likewise defines the offence by objective conduct, covering acts committed deliberately or through negligence, without requiring proof of an intentional element.
    19-d-09.md, ca-19-118807-6253cdd2bd3db21cbdd949b7.md

**12.** An undertaking may therefore be fined for breaking a seal even absent proof of intent, since the breach is deemed to result at least from negligence with only a narrow possibility for the undertaking to rebut that presumption, while the presumption of innocence is preserved because the Commission must first adduce sufficient evidence of the breach before the burden shifts.
    50125.md, 62011CJ0089.md

**13.** Under the Commission's Fines Guidelines, negligence is treated as a mitigating factor in setting the fine only where the undertaking itself has adduced evidence that the infringement was committed negligently.
    35263.md

**14.** In Commission decision of 24 May 2011 in Case COMP/39.796 – Suez Environnement, the Commission fixed a fine of EUR 8,000,000, corresponding to 0.065% of Suez Environnement's 2009 turnover, for a seal breach at the premises of its subsidiary Lyonnaise des Eaux, which the companies argued was unintentional.
    54659.md, at39796.md
