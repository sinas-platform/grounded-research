# Can the Commission inspect a company over conduct a national authority has already investigated? What limits apply, and how have the courts treated the overlap?

**1.** The Commission may choose to proceed by an inspection ordered by decision even without having previously met any opposition from the undertaking, since it enjoys a broad margin of discretion to pick the most appropriate investigative measure, and prior cooperation with an earlier information request does not make such a decision disproportionate.
    inspections-eu-law-2nd--3_chapter_1_-_inspections_upon_decision_regulation_1_2003_article_20_4.md

**2.** In Deutsche Bahn and Others v Commission, C-583/13 P, the Court of Justice held that information obtained through the Commission's inspection powers may be used only for the purpose for which it was acquired, and that where an earlier inspection itself exceeded that scope because Commission agents already held unrelated information, the resulting irregularity is capable of affecting the legality of later inspection decisions founded on it.
    62013CJ0583.md

**3.** Once the Commission initiates proceedings under the first sentence of Article 11(6) of Regulation 1/2003 to adopt a Chapter III decision, the competition authorities of the Member States lose their competence to apply Articles 101 and 102 TFEU against the same undertakings for the same alleged infringement on the same product and geographic markets during the same period.
    62019CJ0857.md

**4.** The European Competition Network's work-allocation mechanism, established by Regulation 1/2003 and integrated by the Commission's 2004 Network Notice, seeks to ensure that cases are conducted by the authority best placed to deal effectively with an alleged infringement.
    92525.md

**5.** In Case C-857/19, the Court of Justice held that ne bis in idem precludes an undertaking from being found liable or proceeded against afresh for anticompetitive conduct for which it has already been penalised or declared not liable by an earlier decision that can no longer be challenged, citing Toshiba Corporation and Others, C-17/10.
    62019CJ0857.md

**6.** As recorded by the referring court in the Advocate General's Opinion in Nordzucker and Others, C-151/20, the Court of Justice's case-law does not yet provide guidance on how the ne bis in idem principle applies where two national competition authorities enforce EU and national law against the same offender on the same facts.
    62020CC0151.md

**7.** The General Court, in Case T-249/17, held that the Commission cannot be required, at the preliminary investigation stage, to disclose anything beyond the presumed infringement it intends to verify, since a broader disclosure duty would upset the balance between investigative efficiency and the rights of defence.
    62017TJ0249.md

**8.** An inspection decision must be directed at gathering evidence to verify the existence and scope of factual and legal situations about which the Commission already possesses sufficiently serious indicia of an infringement, and the EU courts must be satisfied such indicia existed so the decision was not arbitrary.
    62017TJ0249.md

**9.** The General Court has observed that, where a later inspection decision rests on information obtained through earlier inspections, judicial review of that later decision may extend to whether the earlier inspections were themselves lawfully carried out, and decisions built on unlawfully obtained material will be annulled.
    judicial-review-competition-cases--6_european_union_-_judicial_review_of_inspection_decisions.md

**10.** On appeal in Casino, Guichard-Perrachon and Achats Marchandises Casino v Commission, C-690/20 P, the Court of Justice held that the General Court had erred in ruling that the recording obligation under Article 19 of Regulation 1/2003 and Article 3 of Regulation 773/2004 did not apply to supplier interviews, and it annulled the inspection decision because, once that vitiated evidence was excluded, the Commission lacked sufficiently serious indicia to justify it.
    62020CJ0690.md

**11.** The General Court, in France Télécom v Commission, T-340/04, held that the existence of prior Commission orders addressing related conduct did not affect the appropriateness of a subsequent inspection where the Commission possessed information suggesting continued non-compliance with those orders.
    62004TJ0340.md

**12.** The General Court, in Orange v Commission, T-402/13, held that an inspection is not rendered inappropriate merely because the alleged infringement had already been examined by a national competition authority, since the Commission is not bound by a decision of a national court or body applying Articles 101 and 102 TFEU.
    62013TJ0402.md
