# Under what conditions must the European Commission protect personal data during an inspection? Does the GDPR apply? Must documents be sealed on request?

## Conclusion

The Commission must protect personal data whenever an inspection interferes with Charter-protected privacy rights, the GDPR does not itself govern the Commission's own processing of that data during the inspection, and sealing is a security power rather than a right triggered automatically by request.

The Commission must protect personal data whenever an inspection interferes with Charter-protected privacy and data-protection rights, under a strict legal framework and adequate safeguards, including on request.

The GDPR does not itself govern the Commission's own processing of personal data obtained during a competition inspection, though the Commission applies GDPR definitions when handling sensitive personal data supplied to it.

Sealing is a security power available to Commission inspectors during an inspection rather than a measure that must automatically follow upon request.

## Conditions triggering data protection duties

The Commission is bound, while carrying out its tasks, to respect the rights of natural persons in relation to the processing of personal data recognised by Article 8(1) of the Charter and Article 16(1) of the Treaty.[1] Seizure of communications during an inspection without prior judicial authorisation is compatible with Articles 7 and 8 of the Charter only where the applicable legislation and practice provide a strict legal framework and adequate and sufficient safeguards against abuse and arbitrariness, including effective ex post judicial review, as held in Case C-258/23 (IMI – Imagens Médicas Integradas and Others).[2] In Vivendi, Case T-1097/23, the contested decision required documents containing sensitive personal data as defined by the GDPR to be supplied in encrypted format and separately, identified as 'sensitive personal data'.[3] The Court has stressed the Commission's obligation to provide adapted protection of personal data collected during inspections, if so requested.[4] [commentary] Because interference during an inspection is only lawful under a strict legal framework with adequate safeguards, and the Court requires adapted protection where requested, the Commission must protect personal data under these conditions. According to the Opinion, the procedural safeguards competition authorities must apply during inspections align with Article 5 GDPR obligations but are additional to them, and interference with data protection rights is proportionate only where such safeguards are applied.[5] [commentary] In Lagardère, Case T-1119/23, the Commission decision provided procedural safeguards to protect communications covered by professional legal privilege and sensitive personal data.[6]

## Applicability of the GDPR

Article 4(9) GDPR carves out public authorities that receive personal data in the framework of a particular inquiry, such as the Commission during inspections.[4] [commentary]

Since Article 4(9) GDPR excludes the Commission's inquiry-related receipt of data from GDPR obligations, yet the Commission still applies GDPR definitions in its own procedural safeguards, the GDPR does not itself govern the Commission's inspection processing. Where a competition authority identifies an infringement of the GDPR while establishing an abuse of a dominant position, it does not thereby monitor or enforce the GDPR for the purposes of Article 51(1) GDPR, since competition rules and data-protection rules pursue different objectives.[7]

## Mandatory sealing of documents on request

In E.ON Energie, Case T-141/08, officials and other accompanying persons authorised by the Commission to conduct an inspection are empowered to seal any business premises and books or records for the period and to the extent necessary for the inspection.[8] Night seals protect the inspection team's equipment and documents and data collected but not yet indexed, and breaking seals exposes inspected companies to heavy penalties.[9] [commentary] Since sealing is a power inspectors exercise as necessary to secure premises and materials, and no passage shows this power being converted into an obligation triggered by an inspected party's request, sealing on request is not shown to be mandatory.

In Facebook Ireland v Commission, Case T-451/20 R, the President of the General Court ordered that documents likely to contain sensitive personal data be identified by the applicant and examined in a virtual data room accessible to a limited number of the investigation team and the applicant's lawyers, suspending the contested decision's effect until that procedure was in place.[10] In a parallel order in Facebook Ireland v Commission, Case T-452/20 R, the President of the General Court required the applicant to identify documents containing sensitive personal data and communicate them to the Commission on a separate electronic medium, and suspended the contested decision's effect as regards such documents until that procedure was in place.[11]
